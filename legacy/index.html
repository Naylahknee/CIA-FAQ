Already moved in?

The first-month reset

Move-in is over. Now the goal is building a routine before small problems turn into expensive ones.

🧑🏽‍🍳For students

Build your operating system.

  • Check CIA email every day.Schedules, assignments, account notices, and required tasks will not chase you through the family chat.
  • Protect one clean uniform.Keep a complete backup ready for a career event, inspection, spill, or laundry emergency.
  • Learn the point system.Use daily Blue Points before they expire and track Gold Points so weekends do not become a financial jump scare.
  • Save the right contacts.Put Residence Life, technical support, health information, and emergency contacts in your phone now.
  • Ask early.A confusing bill, missing course material, uniform problem, or academic concern is easier to fix before a deadline.
📱For parents

Support without becoming the portal.

  • Agree on a check-in rhythm.Choose a reasonable time instead of treating a quiet afternoon like a missing-person documentary.
  • Use your own access.Proxy access and payment access serve different purposes. Do not use the student’s credentials.
  • Ask what they tried first.Help students identify the correct office, then let them send the email or make the call.
  • Watch the budget, not every meal.Review major account activity and Gold Point use without turning dining choices into daily surveillance.
  • Know the escalation line.Ordinary discomfort builds independence; health, safety, discrimination, or unresolved academic risk needs prompt adult attention.
One useful weekly question:“What is one thing working, one thing confusing, and one thing you need to handle this week?”

Move-in without the meltdown

Your arrival-day game plan

The student’s current Arrival Guide and assigned time always come first.

🧑🏽‍🍳
Student lane

You own the school tasks.

  1. 1
    Before traveling

    Check your CIA email, assigned move-in time, residence hall, room assignment, online orientation, ID-photo task, and required health items.

  2. 2
    Pack the essentials where you can reach them

    Keep your ID, phone charger, medications, arrival instructions, and one complete school-appropriate outfit out of the bottom of the car.

  3. 3
    Inspect before buying duplicates

    Try on uniforms and kitchen shoes, then inventory your program kit. Use the campus exchange process for sizing problems before purchasing replacements.

  4. 4
    Follow your orientation schedule

    Add every required block to your calendar. Do not assume you will be free for shopping, sightseeing, or a long family dinner.

  5. 5
    Know your first-night basics

    Locate dining, your residence-hall support contact, medication, laundry, and the route to your first required activity.

🧳
Parent lane

You support the launch.

  1. 1
    Book around the assigned schedule

    Do not plan travel from an old chat screenshot. Confirm the current arrival instructions before locking in flights, hotels, or the drive home.

  2. 2
    Bring less than you think

    Start with true necessities. Room layouts, roommate plans, and residence-hall rules may make half the “must-have” list unnecessary.

  3. 3
    Handle setup—not school business

    Help unload and organize, but let the student ask staff questions, find offices, read email, and solve one manageable problem independently.

  4. 4
    Expect a real goodbye

    Family and student programming separate. Once the family session ends, students may move directly into orientation activities.

  5. 5
    Leave a calm backup plan

    Agree on when you will check in, where emergency contacts are stored, and how the student will ask for help—then let orientation do its job.

Do not build the weekend around “probably.”Arrival times, family sessions, dining availability, uniform distribution, and orientation schedules can vary by cohort. Students should use the newest instructions sent to their CIA account.

Save this before it is needed

Sick, injured, or out of medication?

For immediate danger or a life-threatening emergency, call 911.

1

Prepare now

Students should save insurance details, allergies, medications, an emergency contact, and the closest pharmacy in their phone. Transfer recurring prescriptions before running low.

2

Choose the right level of care

Use a pharmacy for prescriptions, urgent care for a non-life-threatening problem that cannot wait, and 911 or a hospital for a serious emergency.

3

Call before leaving

Confirm current hours, services, insurance acceptance, and transportation. The uploaded CIA list may not reflect later business changes.
